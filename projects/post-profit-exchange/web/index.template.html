post-profit-exchange

C++ / WebAssembly — bounded exact solver (not AMPL)

Offline research simulation of a store within the exchange. Synthetic demand and costs; no live prices or payments.

The balancing feedback policy responds to actual sales: when accumulated operating contribution is ahead of its funding requirement, prices can fall; when it is short, prices can rise only where forecasts show that raising them helps cover the gap. Fixed worker pay and operating costs are inputs. Forecasting learns from past uncensored sales. Configurable sigma stress informs reserves. Shortfalls produce unfunded Continuity Assurance request records; cash exhaustion ends a path. The fixed-price comparison uses the same consumer draws.

Demand formula, day order, and accounting assumptions
        
Consumers are a standalone synthetic model: potential visitors choose visit/skip; visiting consumers draw budgets, product needs and binary-logit purchase/no-purchase decisions. Purchases are limited by budgets and stock. Product order affects budget allocation. The coefficients are configured research assumptions, not measurements of real customers.

        
The simple exponential smoothing tool (EWMA) forecasts from past price-normalized, uncensored sales. The example starts with empty history and configured demand/error assumptions; no real store observations are supplied. During a run it learns only from past synthetic sales. Every observation is scored before learning. Cold-start status, sigma, prediction bands and forecast errors are inspectable in the day records. Seven eligible observations set a configurable warmup flag; that flag does not establish forecasting accuracy. TFT is a separate tool and is not trained or used here.

        
Customers and forecasts share configured price-response assumptions. Forecasts never see future choices or upcoming demand shocks, but success against this simulator is still conditional on those shared assumptions. It does not measure accuracy against real customers or identify the causal effect of changing prices.

        
Adverse sigma scenarios are conditional stress tests, not guaranteed worst cases. The reserve target combines expected funding shortfall and a conservative joint error allowance over the configured horizon. The schedule charges only additional reserve requirements; releases and earmarks are separate cash movements.

        
Cash funds procurement subject to reserve and current fixed obligations. Acquired batches retain their actual cost; sales and spoilage use FIFO. Operating liquidity may cover temporary forecast shortfalls without becoming an earned-surplus signal. If the optimizer cannot certify coverage, an explicit continuity policy retains current public prices and requests assurance. No assurance payment is invented.

        
The simulation ends for a policy when cash reaches zero after fixed obligations, or obligations remain unpaid. That produces an insolvency record and assurance request. The other comparison path may continue; compare terminal dates as well as totals. A technical model error is reported separately.

        
All operation settings and products are configurable. The native runner loads the central JSON-formatted .cfg and its history JSON, then writes new JSON records without a database. In this standalone page, select both files explicitly; it cannot silently access neighboring files. Export preserves the configuration, history and result.
