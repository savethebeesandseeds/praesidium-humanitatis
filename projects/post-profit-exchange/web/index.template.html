post-profit-exchange

C++ / WebAssembly — bounded exact solver (not AMPL)

Offline research simulation of a store within the exchange. Synthetic demand and costs; no live prices or payments.

The optimized policy maximizes the existing engine's forecast worker surplus at replacement cost under its constraints. The specification's later objective stages are not implemented here. The fixed-price comparison uses the same external demand and cost draws.

Demand formula, day order, and accounting assumptions
        
Demand is a synthetic price response, not measured elasticity. Before integer rounding: base demand × max(0, 1 − slope × (price − reference price) / reference price) × shock factor × variation factor. Slope and factors are divided by 10,000. The C++ implementation truncates the slope quotient and rounds scaled quantities; the exact forecast and realized units are in each daily record.

        
The configured shock is known to that day's forecast before prices are chosen. Forecast scenarios use their three configured factors and probabilities. Realized demand instead uses the seeded per-product noise draw in the range 10,000 ± the noise setting. Both policies receive the same draws; their different prices can produce different demand quantities.

        
Each day replenishes stock toward its target first, using available procurement cash in the listed product order. It protects the existing reserve and known wage and operating obligations from procurement. Changing product order can change purchases when the budget is scarce. Initial stock is an endowment valued at its base unit cost; its value is additional to initial cash.

        
The price decision follows procurement. Each selected price must satisfy the price ceiling and change cap, and all forecast scenarios must fit stock and cover that day's protected obligations at replacement cost. The fixed-price policy faces these same checks. An infeasible day has no trades; purchases, spoilage and wage/operating obligations still occur. Unmet demand on closed days is estimated at the configured reference price.

        
Realized sales are limited by stock, then unsold stock spoils. Sold and wasted units use FIFO acquisition costs. The realized FIFO economic result deducts wages and operating costs due, even when unpaid. Cash pays wage arrears first, then operating arrears. Reserve is earmarked within total cash, is available to cover those obligations, and is not an expense or additional money. New reserve allocations require positive realized result, available cash and no arrears.

        
The optimized policy enumerates at most 729 price combinations per day. It uses the existing expected worker-surplus objective with replacement costs and forecast reserve contribution. The later surplus-target and essential-basket stages remain outside this simulator.
